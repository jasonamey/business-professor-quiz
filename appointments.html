Accountancy

Accountancy studies how organizations measure, record, check, and report financial information, and how investors, managers, tax authorities, and investigators use that information.

Appointment 1 of 25

Professor of Accountancy

specializing in Financial Reporting

How companies present their financial results to investors, lenders, and the public: what goes into financial statements, how items are measured, and who writes the rules.

Research questions
  1. Should intangibles like data, brands, and R&D be capitalized on the balance sheet, or kept off because they're too hard to value reliably?
  2. Is fair-value accounting more informative than historical cost, or does it make volatility worse in a crisis?
  3. Do non-GAAP metrics (“adjusted EBITDA”) help investors, or mainly let managers flatter results?
  4. Should sustainability and climate disclosures be mandatory and standardized, or left to the market?
  5. Would one global set of standards (IFRS everywhere) improve comparability, or erase useful local differences?
Search terms

financial statements · GAAP · IFRS · FASB · IASB · intangible assets · fair value accounting · non-GAAP measures · ESG disclosure · ISSB standards

Appointment 2 of 25

Professor of Accountancy

specializing in Auditing

How independent accountants check whether a company's financial statements are accurate, and how to keep those checkers honest and effective.

Research questions
  1. Will AI and full-population testing improve audit quality, or create risks auditors can't see?
  2. Does mandatory auditor rotation protect independence, or throw away valuable client knowledge?
  3. Should audit firms be allowed to sell consulting services to the clients they audit?
  4. Is the Big Four's concentration a quality advantage or a systemic risk?
  5. Should auditors be responsible for detecting fraud, or only for giving reasonable assurance on the statements?
Search terms

external audit · auditor independence · PCAOB · Sarbanes-Oxley Act · Big Four accounting firms · mandatory audit firm rotation · reasonable assurance · audit expectation gap · audit data analytics

Appointment 3 of 25

Professor of Accountancy

specializing in Taxation

How governments tax businesses, how businesses plan around taxes, and how tax rules shape investment and behavior.

Research questions
  1. Will a global minimum corporate tax end profit shifting, or just move it elsewhere?
  2. Should digital companies be taxed where their users are, or where the company is based?
  3. Do corporate tax cuts boost investment and wages, or mainly shareholder payouts?
  4. Is aggressive tax avoidance a legitimate duty to shareholders or a breach of corporate responsibility?
  5. Should the tax code be used to steer behavior (credits, incentives), or kept simple and neutral?
Search terms

corporate income tax · profit shifting · tax havens · OECD Pillar Two · global minimum tax · digital services tax · Tax Cuts and Jobs Act · tax avoidance vs. tax evasion · tax expenditures

Appointment 4 of 25

Professor of Accountancy

specializing in Managerial Accounting

How organizations use internal financial information, such as costs, budgets, and performance measures, to make decisions and manage people. Unlike financial reporting, this information is for managers rather than outside investors.

Research questions
  1. Do performance metrics improve decisions, or teach people to game the metric?
  2. Is traditional budgeting still useful, or should firms move to rolling forecasts or “beyond budgeting”?
  3. Does activity-based costing justify its complexity?
  4. Should managers be paid on accounting measures or on stock price?
  5. Do balanced scorecards improve strategy, or just add reporting overhead?
Search terms

management accounting · performance measurement · Goodhart's law · budgeting · rolling forecasts · Beyond Budgeting · activity-based costing · balanced scorecard · executive compensation

Appointment 5 of 25

Professor of Accountancy

specializing in Forensic Accounting

How accountants and investigators detect, investigate, and prevent financial fraud, and how they present financial evidence in court.

Research questions
  1. Can analytics and AI detect fraud before it happens, or only after the damage is done?
  2. Do whistleblower bounties uncover fraud, or encourage opportunistic tips?
  3. Are textual signals in financial reports reliable predictors of fraud, such as tone or readability?
  4. Is the “fraud triangle” (pressure, opportunity, rationalization) still an adequate model?
  5. Does crypto make financial crime easier to hide, or easier to trace?
Search terms

forensic accounting · fraud detection · fraud triangle · fraud diamond · SEC whistleblower program · Benford's law · textual analysis of annual reports · blockchain analytics · Association of Certified Fraud Examiners

Economics

Economics studies how people, businesses, and governments make choices with limited resources, and how those choices add up to markets, industries, and national economies.

Appointment 6 of 25

Professor of Economics

specializing in Labor Economics

How labor markets work: wages, employment, and working conditions, and how policies and technology affect workers.

Research questions
  1. Will AI replace workers or make them more productive?
  2. Do minimum wage increases cost jobs, or does the evidence say the effects are small?
  3. Is the gig economy flexible opportunity or precarious work?
  4. Do noncompete agreements protect investment in workers, or suppress wages?
  5. Does immigration lower wages for native workers, or expand the economy for everyone?
Search terms

labor economics · minimum wage · monopsony · gig economy · independent contractor classification · noncompete agreements · immigration and wages · automation and employment · Card and Krueger

Research questions

  3. Was post-pandemic inflation mainly a demand story or a supply story?
  4. Does quantitative easing work, and is unwinding it risky?
  5. Is high government debt dangerous, or manageable when interest rates are low relative to growth?

Federal Reserve · dual mandate · central bank independence · inflation · quantitative easing · government debt · interest rate vs. growth rate (r < g) · monetary policy · fiscal policy
